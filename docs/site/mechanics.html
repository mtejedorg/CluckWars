The loop and the win

Each player picks a class and a specialization, which fixes their four abilities, spawns at their base corner and farms the tiered food piles. Food is carried home and deposited at the base, then the trip repeats. On the way, players steal from loaded rivals, control them, and defend their own haul.

The first player to bank 40 food wins at once. If nobody does, the most food banked when the 45 second timer ends wins.

The stored tie-break order is food, then KOs, then the lower corner index. Players are never told a coin flip decided anything. If food is level at the top and KOs do not settle it, the end screen reads "TIED AT THE TOP!" and every tied first-place bird is crowned. If KOs settle it, the banner stays "{name} WINS!" with "Tied 1st · won on KOs".

The last 10 seconds

Once per round, when 10 playable seconds are left (never during the 3-2-1-GO intro), the match authority rolls one of four events. The HUD shows "FINAL 10!" over its name.

EventEffect
Golden PileA 25-food pile appears near the centre.
Underdog SurgeThe lowest-banked chicken moves ×1.4.
Bounty on the LeaderExecuting the top-banked chicken pays ×2.
RestockEvery pile gets +10, up to its maximum.

Golden Pile and Restock add food beyond the 80-food budget described below. The design document calls this a deliberate late-match exception.

The Solo Clear Time axiom

The balance rule: a naked chicken (no abilities, no passive), alone on the full map and farming greedily, banks the win target in a fixed time, in a fixed number of base trips. Every class stat is solved against that target instead of tuned by feel. Speedy moves at 12.15, not because it feels right, but because Speedy has to clear in 30 seconds.

ClassSolo Clear TimeTripsStrategy
Speedy30 s4Throughput: many small loads, roams wide
Fatty30 s2Payload: one huge sweep, barely returns
Warrior35 s3The ability class: mid stats, leans on the kit
Sneaky40 s4Denial: never farms, robs and executes

Trips are the win target divided by cargo capacity, rounded up. A pure-C# simulator called the Balance Oracle runs the greedy farming policy on the real pile coordinates and checks each class against its target from all four seats. The design band is ±1.5 s. A tighter ±0.5 s guard applies to the shipped asset values.

The map holds 80 food, twice the win target, so two players could in theory max out by farming alone. That is why combat exists: it buys time, and stealing is the only mechanic that turns a won fight directly into food.

Steal and control

All chicken-to-chicken interaction is steal or control. There is no basic attack. A steal moves cargo directly from victim to thief; nothing drops on the floor. The amount is capped three ways:

stolen = min(steal value, free space, defender cargo)   // never negative

A loaded thief has no free space, so it cannot steal. Theft is a rhythm of steal, bank, return, not a drain. A steal-proof bird (an Egg Shell egg) refuses every steal, so the formula never runs against it.

Stun is the only state that locks casting. That is exactly why it is the qualifier for the Assassin's execute. The Egg Shell egg is immune to the whole ladder, while the Egg Shell walk is immune to ability slows only; steal-proofing is separate and only stops cargo being taken. A Relentless caster lengthens the stuns and slows it applies by 30% (a Relentless stun lasts 1.69 s), applied before the victim's own shortening; roots and placed zones are not lengthened.

Death Mark: one button, two presses

The Assassin's execute is the only hard removal in the game. Only the Assassin specialization has it, in the first of the four ability slots. The press marks a rival anywhere on the map for 4 s, picked at random but likelier the farther it is from its own base and, softly, from the Assassin; the Assassin must find it, stun it and press again within 6.2 m, in the last second of the mark. Each round opens with Death Mark on a 4 s cooldown. No ability crosses a claimed base wall, so a bird at home cannot be reached and the mark can run out.

StageConditionCounterplay
MarkFirst press marks a living rival anywhere on the map, likelier one far from its own base. The target can see the mark.Reach your base in time: no ability crosses a base wall.
HuntThe Assassin has 4 seconds to find it and close in. The mark arms after 3 s: a Kill is only possible in the last 1.0 s.Escape: stay out of reach until the timer runs out, or walk home (a victim within about 21 to 29 m of home is safe).
StunThe marked target must be stunned when the Kill lands, from any source (a stun that began before the arm and is still running counts). Stun lasts 1.3 s (Slippery too), 0.845 s on Bulwark.Don't get stunned.
KillPress again in the last second, within 6.2 m, while the target is stunned, not shelled, and the Assassin is not stunned.—

No isolation: the mark ignores crowds. The only defences are not being stunned, escaping, and reaching your base in time. An Egg Shell's egg (the last 2 of its 3 seconds) makes you unkillable and unstunnable; its first-second walk does not. Stun reach (5.4 m) plus kill range (6.2 m) is 11.6 m.

On success the victim's whole cargo moves to the Assassin as a bounty bag that ignores the capacity limit. The bag can be stolen like any other load: a steal takes the beak first, then the bag. The victim is out for 3 seconds, then respawns at its own base with no cargo; its cooldowns keep running. If the execute fails, Death Mark takes a reduced cooldown (4 s) so the Assassin can re-mark quickly; after a kill it is 8 s, so a round holds at most four kills.

What each player sees: the Assassin gets one marker in the target's own colour (a skull, a draining ring and the distance), a skull on the target's standings row, and a big yellow KILL button exactly while a kill is possible (until the arm the marker shows a countdown digit and KILL stays dark). The marked player gets a MARKED! warning, a darkened screen for as long as the mark lasts, a clock pill reading LIVE IN 3, 2, 1 while the mark arms and then a red, pulsing LIVE 1s, and a skull on the Assassin's arrow; when the Assassin is close the darkness turns cold violet. Everyone else sees only the skull on the standings, which turns red and pulses when the mark goes live. A sting plays at the arm for each role.

The 10 abilities

Simplified on 2026-11-05: one ability per job, with plain names. Every targeted ability hits every rival within its radius of you, by distance only: there is no cone and no aiming at a single rival. Nineteen older moves are retired (Quick Drop's banking bonus is now a permanent Featherfoot passive). Each player has four active slots, filled by the kit of the chosen specialization. Hold to aim, release to use

An ability always fires on release, never on press. The moment you press, your own screen shows the move's true area on the ground and marks every chicken inside it. What is marked is exactly what gets hit: with range-only targeting that is every rival inside a circle around you.

Past about 125 ms the hold becomes a commitment. Rivals see a wind-up glow at your feet, though not your area, and the stick rotates your aim instead of moving you.

Having no target is not illegal. A move that needs a rival and finds none shows a cream dashed ring. Release it anyway and it fizzles: no effect, no cooldown, and the slot re-arms after 0.4 s so it cannot be spammed. A held move can be cancelled without firing or costing its cooldown: drag to the screen edge on touch, Esc or right mouse button on keyboard and mouse, B on a gamepad.

Piles, walls and jumps

Tiered piles

TierPilesFood eachTotalRole
T1 doorstep4520Safe income near your base
T2 contested41040Equidistant from two bases
T3 centre12020The prize

That is 80 food in total, twice the win target. Piles do not respawn, so banked food leaves circulation and the pot visibly shrinks as the match goes on.

Walls

Eight radial walls divide the arena into eight 45 degree sectors, alternating between base sectors and neutral sectors. Each wall has exactly one opening, at one end. The pattern repeats four times, so every player's sector is identical. From your base, the left route is the safe lap: out to the rim, along the edge, past a contested pile. The right route is the risky lap: in past the centre pile.

Jumps

Every jump is a teleport, and there are no obstacle height classes. A jump clears an obstacle only if its length beats the obstacle's span plus 0.8 m of body clearance. A jump that falls short stops flush against the near face, and the cooldown is still spent.

Jumps come in a ladder of lengths. The shortest closes the gap over a wall, a longer one vaults a pile, and only the longest crosses a full centre pile. The tier lengths are still being tuned, so this page does not list them.

The centre pile shrinks as it empties, and since span is the only thing gating a jump, the map opens up in stages. A full pile is a fortress that only the longest jump crosses. As it shrinks, shorter jumps can cross it, and a nearly empty pile blocks nothing. The prize everyone is fighting over is also the wall protecting it, so the arena dissolves as the match converges.

An open problem

The arena is too big for four players in 45 seconds. In play, four chickens rarely run into each other, so a whole match can pass in parallel: everyone farms, everyone banks. The control and steal systems that the v0.4 redesign exists to serve go unexercised. The design document marks this as known, accepted for now, and something that must be solved before the map is called done.

The levers it lists, none chosen yet:

  • Shrink the arena. Cheapest, but every spatial constant derives from its size, and shrinking alone shortens Solo Clear Time unless speeds come down too.
  • Pull the objectives inward, so routes overlap sooner.
  • Raise the player count. The arena may simply be sized for more players, but that changes the free-for-all maths and the win target.
  • Add interior geometry that funnels traffic through shared corridors.

The design document's reason for waiting: it is not yet known whether the encounter rate is wrong or the readability of encounters is, and guessing costs a re-solve of every derived constant. This is the first question a human playtest has to answer.
