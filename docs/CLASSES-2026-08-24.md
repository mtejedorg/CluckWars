# 1. There is no HP, no damage, no basic attack

All chicken-to-chicken interaction is **control + steal**. Encounters are decided by ability choice, cooldown management and positioning — catching a *loaded* rival mid-route beats winning a fair fight. Durability is expressed through control resistance (via specializations) and defensive abilities, never a health bar.

**Speedy and Assassin share Move Speed and Cargo but clear in 30s vs 40s** — the whole gap is Collection Rate (3.0 vs 1.7). The Assassin is a fast chicken who is *genuinely bad at farming*; that's the mechanical reason he denies instead of collects — he cannot even equip Peck.

## 2.1 The four essences

Each class is a mechanical contract, not just a stat spread — an ability is in the wrong class if it doesn't serve this:

| Class | Essence | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Assassin** | "Stealing from others, isolating them, catching them, killing from the shadows." | Every tool needs a victim. Setup → payoff, binary outcomes, high skill ceiling. The only class that cannot forage — it eats what others carried. |
| **Speedy** | "The fastest one. Pick all the grain in the shortest time. Never jump obstacles — it would be unfair." | Tempo and denial-of-pursuit. **Never gets terrain traversal, under any circumstance** — enforced in code, not just convention. It already answers escape (highest Move Speed) and denial (three Control abilities); traversal would be a third currency for free. |
| **Warrior** | "The bruiser. Good for all, expert in nothing. Spams abilities." | Short cooldowns, broad coverage, every effect deliberately weaker than the specialist's version — that's a number, not flavour text. |
| **Fatty** | "Slow but unstoppable, carries no matter what. Toolbox is opportunity and control." | Area denial, punish, cargo resilience. Any leap must read as launched mass, not agility. |

# 3. Specializations — built and shipped

Every class offers **exactly two specializations**, chosen once at character select. Each grants a permanent passive and, for five of the eight, force-equips a **signature ability** into one of the four active slots (see §4 for why that matters).

| Class | Specialization | Passive | Signature (forced) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Warrior** | The Brute (Bully) | Steals bigger, + cargo room to hold it | **Scrap** — steal on contact |
|  | The Relentless | Abilities return faster | **Headbutt** — shove + stagger, low cooldown |
| **Speedy** | The Agile (Slippery) | Control effects wear off 40% faster | ⏳ pending — a Peck variant |
|  | The Anxious (Featherfoot) | Immune to the pile-slow | ⏳ pending — a Peck variant |
| **Fatty** | The Hauler (Hoarder) | Carries at least a full win's worth | ⏳ pending — a Peck variant |
|  | The Boulder (Bulwark) | Shorter control, 75% less knockback | **Ground Quake** — stomp roots the area |
| **Assassin** | The Reaper (Spoiler) | Banks a bonus if the clock expires with no winner | **Mark/Kill** — the execute, §5 |
|  | The Burglar (Thief) | Every steal takes 1.6x more | **Sneaky Steal** |

> [!warning] ⏳
> **Three signatures are pending, not missing.** Slippery, Featherfoot and Hoarder each want a *Peck variant* (empty-beak rush, burst-fed, heavy-beakful) as their signature. Peck's amount and cooldown feed Solo Clear Time directly, so these must be solved against the balance Oracle rather than hand-tuned — landing them is the next roster milestone. Until then, those three specializations grant their passive only.

**Why each signature is amplified only by its own passive:** Bully makes Scrap's theft worth taking (bigger steal + the cargo room to carry it); Relentless makes Headbutt genuinely spammable. Neither signature is impressive under the *other* passive, on purpose — that asymmetry is what makes the choice read as a build, not a stat tweak.

**Visual identity per specialization is designed but not built.** Speedy's Agile/Anxious split has a concrete brief (upright-and-still vs. crouched-and-ruffled); concept art exists and needs regenerating in the shipped house style before it goes to 3D.

# 4. Loadout rules — 4 active slots, always

- **Every class has exactly 4 active ability slots.** There is no separate passive slot outside the specialization choice.
- Two independent rules can **force** up to 2 of the 4:
  1. **Peck is force-equipped for every class that can forage** — Warrior, Speedy, Fatty. The Assassin never gets it, under any circumstance.
  2. **The chosen specialization's signature, if it has one, is also force-equipped.** If that signature is itself a Peck *variant*, it **replaces** plain Peck instead of taking a second slot. A non-Peck signature (Scrap, Headbutt, Ground Quake, Mark/Kill, Sneaky Steal) costs a slot on top of Peck.
- This is why free choice differs by class today: a forager whose specialization has no signature yet picks 3 of 4; one whose specialization has a non-Peck signature picks 2 of 4; the Assassin, which never carries Peck, picks 3 of 4 either way.
- **The Common pool is exactly two abilities: Egg Shell (every class) and Peck (Warrior/Speedy/Fatty).** It used to also hold Speed Burst and Snatch — both turned out to be class essences hiding in the shared pool (Speed Burst is Speedy's entire "fastest in the game" claim; Snatch is an AoE steal, which is the Assassin's whole income) and were moved to their own class.
- **A Character ability belongs to exactly one class, full stop.** Sharing is expressed only by the Common slot now, never by a wider class mask on a Character ability.

# 5. The execute — Mark/Kill

The only hard removal in the game, and it belongs to one specialization only.

| Stage | Condition | Counterplay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mark** | Press → soft-locks the nearest isolated rival (no chicken within 8m), in facing. Visible to the target. | Stay near others — in an FFA, safety = approaching an enemy. |
| **Arm** | 2.0s pass with the mark alive. | Break isolation or leave range → mark fizzles. |
| **Qualify** | The marked target enters a stun (his own Ambush, or any stun — e.g. a Warrior's Wing Slam) → button lights up **KILL**. | Don't get stunned; hold a defence. |
| **Kill** | Press again while the target is stunned and the Assassin is not himself stunned. | A third party can stun the Assassin to deny the kill — stun the hunter to save the prey. |

On success the victim's entire cargo transfers to the Assassin as a capacity-exempt "bounty bag" and the victim is removed ~2s, then respawns empty. **Mark/Kill is the Reaper specialization's forced signature** — choosing Reaper equips it automatically; the Burglar specialization does not carry it and cannot execute.
