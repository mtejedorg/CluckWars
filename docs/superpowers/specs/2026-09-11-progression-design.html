Overview

Everything the player keeps when the match ends.

Cluck Wars has no progression system today. This designs the whole meta layer — identity, goals, rewards, rhythm and social — plus the plan to build it, with a pitch milestone at slice 4. The game announces what happened; progression listens. The match itself is never modified.

The loop, between rounds

Click a beat to see what answers it. Every one is a system below, and they are ordered by how a real session actually unfolds.

Why the economy came second

A first pass at this designed only currency and rank, and it read as an economy rather than a progression system. The reason is worth stating: currency is connective tissue, not content. A player does not return for a number — they return for a goal, a record they nearly have, a collection with a gap in it, and an identity that says something true about how they play.

So the ordering here is deliberate. Identity first, then goals, then the rewards that feed them. Grain and Flock Rank are plumbing that makes the rest legible.

At the pitch a player can earn, unlock, set goals, see their record and build an identity — all from one local file, with no backend and nothing to operate. Everything after is scale on a loop that already works.

The systems

Identity, goals, rewards, rhythm

Seven systems, in the order a player meets them. Each is a fold or a predicate over the same six-verb event stream, which is why the whole layer costs far less than the list suggests.

1 · The nameplate

Assembled from four parts and shown everywhere a player is represented — lobby, scoreboard, results, friends list. Click through the options; the source of each part is what matters.

Every nameplate carries at least one part that cannot be bought.

A stranger reading your plate learns something true about how you play. That single constraint is what stops the identity layer reading as a billboard — and it is why titles and emblems are earn-only while banners are the purchasable slot.

Why this comes before the economy

A skin in a game with no profile is a recolour nobody sees. The nameplate is the canvas that turns every other reward into something worth having. It also means a small catalogue recombines — around thirty parts produce hundreds of distinguishable identities, which matters enormously when the art pipeline is the real constraint.

2 · Records and titles

Permanent, never expiring, no countdowns. Each is a predicate over the event stream, so authoring cost is near zero — and each awards a title, which lands on the nameplate.

Filter to “achievable while losing” — most of the list survives, and several are easier that way. A denial game generates steal statistics precisely because you are not winning on banking.

Three deliberate omissions

  • No “play 100 rounds” records. Time served is not a feat, and a record that only measures attendance devalues the ones that measure skill.
  • No hidden records. The unearned list is the content — it is what a player browses when deciding whether to keep playing.
  • No expiry. A record you can miss forever is a countdown wearing a trophy.

3 · Career and history

Lifetime totals, a per-class breakdown, a recent-twenty placement strip, personal bests, and the specialization split. It compares the player against their own past and never against strangers.

A global leaderboard tells 99% of players they are nobody. A personal best gives every player something they can beat.

This is also the honest raw material for “you improved” — a claim that only lands when it is measured against a number the player recognises as their own.

Never show a stat that shames. No “you were the only player who banked nothing”, no comparative percentile against the population. The career page is a record, not a verdict.

4 · Weekly goals and the daily task

Three goals a week, generated from a handful of templates with threshold tiers. Every one is stat-based, outcome-blind and cumulative across the week — placement is never mentioned, so all of them are completable in rounds you lose.

Three deliberate omissions, and their replacements

  • No countdown. The refresh day is shown; a ticking timer never is. A visible countdown converts an objective into an obligation.
  • No wins required. At a 25% win rate a win-goal fails most players through no fault of their own.
  • No streak. A streak works by loss aversion — it makes not playing cost something, which is hostile to a game whose entire pitch is that a loss costs nothing.

The daily first-game task

One small single-round task a day — bank 15, steal 5, land two control abilities — shown before your first round. It is the daily reason to open the game, and it pays the daily bonus.

It stays open all day. If it only counted in the first round, a bad first round would burn the day's chance — which makes a loss cost something, the one thing this design refuses. Framed around your first game; failing that game costs nothing. It is hidden while the onboarding ramp runs, because the ramp's own objectives already do that job.

Alongside it, rested ×1.5 on the first three rounds each day. Both are purely additive: skipping a day loses nothing you had.

5 · The onboarding ramp

Everyone starts on the same class with its complete kit, learns the game as that chicken, then chooses their second. Click through the steps — each grant was chosen for what it teaches, not for what it is worth.

Warrior is the tutorial class, and the Assassin is disqualified by construction

The Assassin cannot take Peck at all, so it literally cannot perform the loop the tutorial teaches — and it sits outside the balance model entirely. Fatty is two trips, too few repetitions to teach a loop. Speedy learns farm-and-run and never meets steal or control. Warrior is mid on every axis and its pool is the only one spanning steal, control and utility.

Breadth, not depth

A new player holds a complete kit on one class, not a fraction of a kit. What they lack is roster options — counterpicking and variety, not moment-to-moment capability. In a game with no health bars where every fight is control and theft, a full kit on one class is a real kit. This is the property the ramp's safety rests on.

Must

  • Auto-complete each objective after 3 attempts — ceiling 12 rounds
  • Show locked content, labelled with the step that unlocks it
  • Resolve unlocks at the results screen, never mid-round

Must not

  • Put ramp state on the network — a “this one is new” marker in a 4-player free-for-all is a targeting instruction
  • Price locked content — a step name says keep playing, a price says pay us
  • Be skippable, by currency or anything else
  • Ever run twice, on any device

7 · Rhythm, return, and social

Return hooks — all additive, none breakable

The daily first-game task · rested multiplier · the weekly goal refresh · a “while you were away” summary on a three-day-plus lapse that leads with what you own · personal-best moments · friend activity.

Notifications: one per day maximum, off by default, and only for something unpredictable. “Your goals refreshed” is not news — it happens on schedule and the player knows it.

Refused outright: win streaks, login streaks, timed offers, double-XP windows. Each makes losing or not playing cost something, which deletes the pitch rather than adding retention to it.

Seasons — architected, not shipped

The only thing built now is that Flock Rank reads its ladder from a data asset, so a season is a second asset rather than a second system. Four commitments made now so a later season cannot drift: points reset but rank is retained; nothing gameplay-relevant is ever seasonal; items return in later rotations rather than being gone forever; and no countdown shorter than a season, never on the results screen.

Social — friends and quick chat

Nothing a player sends can be anything other than a phrase you wrote.

Unmoderatable by construction. No filter to tune, no report queue, no rate limiting, no message logs to retain and delete, and nobody who has to be available to answer abuse reports. It translates once, like any other UI string.

WherePhrasesPurpose
Friends list“Play with me?” · “Rematch?” · “One more”The invite — the one thing chat genuinely must do here
Lobby“Hello!” · “Good luck” · “Ready”Acknowledging the humans you are about to rob
Results“Well played” · “Nice steal” · “So close”The 45-second game's whole social vocabulary

Expression during a round is already covered by emotes, which are collectible and so double as a reward. Usernames are generated — SwiftBeak#2213 is thematic, unique without a uniqueness check, and impossible to make offensive. A chosen name would be the only free-text surface left in the product; the recommendation is to ship generated names and add chosen ones only if players ask.

Friend leaderboards, not global ones. A top-hundred board is unwinnable for almost everyone; a five-friend board is winnable by everyone in it.

Why the ratio is the design

In a four-player free-for-all the median player loses 75% of rounds by construction, not by being bad. Around 2:1, winning is clearly better and losing is clearly still worth the 45 seconds. Past 3:1 you rebuild the sting the short round exists to remove; toward 1:1 outcome stops mattering.

Try the second row — an Assassin who banks almost nothing and robs everyone. That player is doing the harder thing and the economy should say so, while the cap stops one dramatic robbery from outpaying a whole round of good play.

It is also the anti-idling system, for free

Participation pays for finishing, so a quitter gets nothing, and neither the bank nor the steal term pays anything for standing still. No separate detection is needed.

Flock Rank

One track, fed by every Grain earned. The curve responds to whatever the calculator above says you earn per day.

Cost of each level Cumulative XP
Above level 20 every level costs the same, permanently.

A level at 60 costs what a level at 20 cost, so a veteran's twenty minutes is never worth less than a newcomer's. What tapers is reward density — early levels pay a cosmetic each, later ones alternate — but the earn rate never drops. There is no wall, because walls exist to convert frustration into purchases and nothing here is sold to relieve frustration.

The first reward lands around round four. If the first payoff does not arrive inside the first session and the first few minutes, the player never learns the system exists.

Two wallets that never touch

 GrainFeathers
SourceRounds onlyPurchase, plus 25 per Flock Rank level

ConvertsNeverNever

The split tracks production cost, which is the honest thing a paid currency should measure. A recolour is a material swap; a new rigged mesh is a full art-and-rigging pipeline run. That makes the shop defensible to players and removes the need to arbitrate item by item.

Feathers cannot ship as a single integer.

Purchased currency is not digital content, so unused purchased balance carries a 14-day withdrawal right. With one integer you cannot answer “how many purchased Feathers are still unspent” and will either over-refund or refuse a refund you owe. Two ledgers, spending granted first and then purchased oldest-first. A seam requirement — retrofitting touches save data, the cloud schema, and every grant site.

The one number that cannot be frozen yet

Everything above is per round, but session yield depends on how fast rounds cycle — and that is a matchmaking question, not a progression one. Cycling in place is roughly 51 seconds; returning to matchmaking between rounds is closer to 90. That is nearly a 2× swing in the whole economy, decided elsewhere.

The constants are shaped correctly and the ratios hold either way. Sign off the absolute values once session flow is settled.

What checking the code changed

Round end is polled, not detected

Solo runs in GameMode.Single, where a ChangeDetector can silently skip locally-written state — PlayerBase already documents this and polls instead. Detecting round end the obvious way would drop rewards only in solo: exactly the pitch build.

Your peer runs the bots

In solo, bot steals and deposits execute on your machine and reach the announcement interface. So the tracker records every chicken per round and RoundEnded names which one was you — which also makes “rob the round's leader” free.

Steals had no chokepoint

Five sites credited the thief by hand. One ReceiveStolen method now credits and announces, and a test fails if any steal path writes cargo directly — so a sixth steal ability cannot silently under-pay.

The boundary is a test, not a compiler rule

Everything compiles into one assembly, so nothing stops gameplay importing progression. Source-scanning tests enforce it instead, a pattern the suite already uses for steal RPCs. With no JSON package installed, the journal is JSON Lines via Unity's built-in serializer.

The two slices that change the pitch

Slices 3 and 4 — identity and goals. No backend, no store, no compliance work, no ongoing operation. Between them they deliver a profile, a nameplate, records, a career, daily goals and the onboarding ramp — everything a player experiences as “this game has progression”. Both are folds over a local journal.

Everything before them is plumbing and everything after is scale — which is exactly why the pitch milestone ends at slice 4.

Deliberately not in the plan

Global leaderboards — a board fed by self-reported peer-to-peer results is a cheat magnet, and unlike a private wallet, cheating it is visible to everyone, which damages trust in the game rather than just the economy. A battle pass — a thin pass is worse than none and one cannot be fed monthly yet. An off-the-shelf economy service — its value is server-authoritative balances, unavailable here anyway.

Standing rules

Directions that outlive this plan

Each of these is the natural next thing somebody will ask for. Written down while nothing is sold, they cost nothing. Written down afterwards, they are an argument. These belong in the project conventions, not just here.

The boundary

The game announces. Progression listens. Gameplay never learns that progression exists.

A handful of places that already bank resources, land steals and end rounds each gain a single additive line. Nothing branches on the result, nothing waits for it, and a build without progression binds a do-nothing implementation. That one interface is the entire coupling surface between the two halves of the project.

// One interface. Injected into gameplay. Null implementation when progression is off.
IMatchEventSink

  RoundStarted      ( ruleset )
  ResourceBanked    ( actorId, amount )                    — ChickenCargo.FlushBaseDeposit
  ResourceStolen    ( actorId, victimActorId, amount )     — ChickenCargo.ReceiveStolen
  OpponentDisabled  ( actorId )                            — ChickenMatchStats.RPC_CreditKill
  AbilityResolved   ( actorId, abilityKey, connected )     — AbilityController.TryActivate
  RoundEnded        ( standings, localActorId, duration )  — GameManager, polled

Six verbs carry the entire meta layer. Every objective, record, career stat and currency award is a predicate or an accumulation over that stream. Nothing downstream needs a seventh.

  • Announcements are local. Each fires on the peer owning the action; nothing is sent over the network, so none can desync a round or cost bandwidth.
  • Nothing branches on them. No return value, no await. Unheard, an announcement is a virtual call into an empty method.
  • The ruleset travels with the round, so a result recorded today stays interpretable after the rules change.

Progression may

  • Subscribe to the announcement interface
  • Accumulate what it hears into its own state
  • Own the shell — menu, character select, lobby, profile
  • Gate what the picker offers
  • Add a results line to the existing panel

Progression may not

  • Read or write gameplay state directly
  • Send anything back into a running round
  • Change the round lifecycle, timing or restart flow
  • Touch anything the balance solver reads
  • Alter bot behaviour or difficulty
  • Grant anything that changes a stat, ever

The rule with no exceptions

Class stats are solved against a per-class clear-time target, not hand-tuned. A reward as small as “+2% collection rate at Flock Rank 10” puts an unsolved number into a live round and makes the balance model a fiction. It is also exactly how a cosmetic economy turns into pay-to-win. Refused on both grounds — so everything progression grants is identity.

Storage: a journal, not a balance

Record what happened. Never record what it earned.

Each round appends a record of facts — banked, stolen, placement, class, duration, and the ruleset in force. The wallet, rank, records and unlocks are all computed by folding that journal. They are derived state, never the source of truth.

The write order is the design: evaluate, then append and flush to disk, then fold and raise events, then fire-and-forget sync. Four things this buys:

  • Offline play works properly. Twenty rounds with no network, then signing in on a second device, resolves as a union of both journals by round id, re-folded. The fold is order-independent and ignores duplicates.
  • Rewards cannot be silently dropped. A sync failure is then a legitimate no-op — the network is down, that is the game working. Only a disk failure is a real error, and that one must reach the player, not a log nobody reads mid-playtest.
  • Records added later credit historical play. A record shipped in six months evaluates over the whole journal, so a player who already did the thing gets it immediately. Store only a balance and every new record starts everyone at zero.
  • The trust boundary can move without an economy reset. A server later re-runs the same pure function over the same facts, and every historical round is re-checkable.

Do not pretend obfuscation is protection

Sign the local file if you like, but only ever as detection. A team that documents obfuscation as anti-tamper stops thinking about the real problem. When a server does land, the cheapest effective check is a clock, not a physics model — rounds cycle roughly every 54 seconds, so no honest account submits more than about 66 an hour.

Conflicts resolve in the player's favour

Spend the same Grain on two devices while offline and the merge honours both unlocks, floors the wallet at zero, and writes an entry recording the shortfall. Revoking content someone is looking at is far worse than a small leak.

Compliance — five rules

1 · No Grain↔Feather conversion, in either direction, ever.

European guidance excludes currencies obtainable solely through gameplay from its scope entirely — which is what keeps Grain out of it. The moment an exchange exists, Grain becomes purchasable by another route and drops back in. This single rule is the difference between a defensible one-hop design and the multi-hop structure that drew a $20M settlement elsewhere.

2 · Real-money equivalents always shown, always at the base rate.

Every Feather price renders with its euro equivalent adjacent, computed from the smallest pack — never the best-value one. Most storefronts naively use the discounted rate, which understates the true cost. All prices and drips sit on a 50-unit grid so nobody is left holding a stranded balance, and an exact top-up is always available.

3 · Every item also carries a direct real-money price.

Buy this skin for €4.49, no currency involved. Regulators have ordered this as a remedy elsewhere; building it from the start turns a remedy into a defence. Pair it with a remote flag that switches Feather sales off while the store keeps working — if the rules tighten, that is an afternoon rather than a rebuild.

4 · Real-money spending is off by default.

A cartoon chicken game will be played by children whether or not they are the audience, and “we didn't target them” is not a defence anyone accepts. Spending unlocks only through a parental gate. No countdown offers, ever. Use the platform age-signal API rather than collecting birthdates — asking for a birthdate creates the consent obligation you are trying to satisfy.

5 · No free text anywhere. Hold this line.

With quick chat instead of a message box there is nothing to filter, nothing to report, no message logs to retain and later delete, and no children's communications to answer for. The friends graph is still personal data and still needs consent and an age bracket — but that is a form and a flag, not an operations team. A text box added later reinstates every obligation this rule removes.

Not legal advice. Get a Spanish consumer-law review before the first euro moves, and cover the friends graph in the same pass.

Remote config, in one line

It may change what things cost and how fast you earn them. It may never change what happens inside a round.

Two independent reasons. A remotely-altered match rule means the balance model and the live game disagree while every test still passes. And in a peer-to-peer session, a client with a stale config simulates different rules from its opponents — that is a desync, not a balance question.

Risks & calls

What was decided, and what could still bite

The decision log first, because most risks below are consequences of a call rather than accidents. Every one of these was made with its cost visible.

Decision log

Accepted risks

Withdrawal-right engineeringMost underestimated

A real 14-day refund flow with tranche accounting and a separate consent click is work most solo developers never do — and they are quietly non-compliant as a result. It must be in the data model from day one; retrofitting touches save data and every grant site.

The pressure to add a text boxWatch this one

Quick chat will feel limiting to somebody within a week of launch, and “just a small text field” is the most reasonable-sounding request in this whole document. It is not small: it reinstates the filter, the report queue, the retention policy and the staffing in a single commit. The phrase set is the compliance posture — treat widening it as a design change and adding free text as a company decision.

A dropped announcement is silentCost of the event model

A future refactor that moves the deposit path and forgets the emit produces no error, no warning, and a quietly under-paying economy. Mitigated by a test per site, which is why the announcement sites are their own build slice rather than an afterthought.

A new player meets veterans mid-rampAccepted

The ramp does not gate matchmaking, so a day-three player can face a full roster. Largely defused by the full-kit choice — the gap is breadth, not power — but it is the first thing to watch in a real playtest rather than something to assume away.

Two currencies in one gameArgued, not immune

Mixing currencies is named in European guidance. The defence is that only one is purchasable and there is no exchange between them — a genuinely good argument, and one that evaporates the instant anyone ships a conversion.

Tightening EU rules on in-game currencyReal, long-dated

Proposed legislation has not landed and realistic binding effect is years out. The worst-case mitigations — a direct real-money path and a kill switch — are already compliance rules 3 and 4. A reason to build the compliant version, not evidence the two-wallet decision was wrong.

Open, and owned elsewhere

  • Round cadence — the ~2× economy swing described in Numbers. Belongs to the matchmaking thread.
  • The tutorial scene — step 0's scripted intro. Nothing resembling it exists in the project; it feeds progression but is not progression, and deserves its own thread.
  • Chosen versus generated usernames — the last free-text decision, currently recommended as generated.

What this plan closes

The currency earn formula has been an open design question for the life of the project. This proposes an actual answer, with every constant isolated in one asset so the whole economy can be retuned from a single place after the first real playtest — rather than hunted down across a dozen files.
